## Container and stream observations

OpenCV reports one decodable video stream with observed FourCC `h264`, 1344 ×
760 decoded BGR frames, nominal 180 fps, 13,766 declared and 13,766 decoded
frames. The last decoded presentation time is 76.472222 s. File-size divided
by this duration gives approximately 24,094 kbps. Stream count, container time
base, DTS, codec profile, and source pixel format were unavailable through the
available OpenCV interface and remain `UNKNOWN`; they were not guessed.

The derived frame set contains 2,295 frames at every sixth source frame (about
30 Hz), in original order. Images are decoded color frames re-encoded as JPEG
quality 95 for diagnostics; the MP4 remains the source of truth.

The exposure statistics show a generally dark indoor image with limited

The lowest Laplacian interval is around 38.47 s; the lowest sampled feature

The spatial feature grid is strongly uneven: the lower two rows are often
empty (floor/low-texture view), while the upper and central rows contain most
features. A static lower-frame mask is therefore a possible future experiment,
but no mask was applied to this dataset.

## Motion, rolling shutter, and compression

Sampled Lucas–Kanade optical flow gives a median displacement of 5.57 px,
P95 19.85 px, P99 29.93 px, and maximum 58.73 px between approximately
30-Hz diagnostic samples. This is image motion only; it is not metric camera
motion or visual odometry. High-flow diagnostic runs are recorded in
`intervals.json`/`intervals.yaml`, including intervals near 29.6–30.6 s and
72.0–73.0 s.

No numerical rolling-shutter readout time can be recovered. The decoded video
contains no independent geometric reference that would support a calibrated
rolling-shutter estimate; qualitative inspection remains observational.
The H.264 context and approximately 24 Mbps bitrate should be retained when
interpreting results. This pass did not establish a quantitative block/ringing
score; direct NV21/raw comparison is deferred to VSL-1B.

## Diagnostic intervals and outputs

`intervals.json` and `intervals.yaml` contain contiguous sampled annotations
for low sharpness, high optical-flow proxy, low ORB diagnostic count, and
extreme luminance candidates. They are evidence labels, not flight ground
truth. Representative images (sharpest, lowest sharpness, darkest, brightest,
and highest sampled flow) are under `quality/examples/`; plots are under
`quality/plots/`.

Machine-readable outputs include:

- `video_stream.json` — provenance, stream observations, timing statistics, and integrity counters;
- `timing.csv` — every decoded frame index, encoded PTS, interval, duplicate, and decode status;
- `quality/frame_quality.csv` — sampled image metrics and spatial feature counts;
- `intervals.json` / `intervals.yaml` — diagnostic interval manifest;
- `provenance.yaml` and `analysis_complete.json` — derivation and completion record.

## VSL-1B direct-recording follow-up

The existing implementation already exposes the required metadata. Reuse
`tools/os04a10_highfps/run_device.py`, `official_capture.cpp`,
`vin_nv21_frame.hpp`, `FrameMetadata`, `FrameContinuity`, and the existing
`HighFpsPipeline` logs. The next controlled recording should use the verified
driver build and a new output directory, for example:

```bash
python3 -B tools/os04a10_highfps/run_device.py \
  --driver-build <verified-build-directory> \
  --official-mode full180 --nv21 --itp-depth 4 --queue-depth 4 \
  --system-media-lib --remote-root /root/os04a10-tests \
  --seconds 60 --business-config config/green_detector_full180.conf
```

The resulting `frames.csv` records `sequence,pts_raw,monotonic_us,width,
height,stride,frame_bytes,format,get_us,hold_us,loop_gap_us,cpu`; `business.json`
records sequence anomalies and latest-slot replacements; `leases.json` records
borrowed-buffer balance; and `vision.csv`, `motion.csv`, and `targets.jsonl`
retain processing/output timing. Keep `pts_raw` unchanged with unknown unit,
clock, and event semantics until independently verified. Also preserve
`capture.json`, camera settings, health/MIPI snapshots, build hashes, and the
exact command. This is sufficient to measure producer cadence, application
visible sequence behavior, queue replacement, lease lifetime, and recording
overhead without creating another camera driver.

## What this MP4 proves and does not prove

It proves that this encoded artifact can be decoded completely, has a regular
encoded presentation timeline at the observed 180 fps, contains no detected
exact/frozen runs under the stated diagnostics, and provides measurable image
texture, luminance, blur proxies, and image motion across 76.47 s.

It does not prove exposure timestamps, VIN/device PTS scale, host or sensor
latency, pre-encoder sensor drops, IMU synchronization, calibration quality,
SLAM accuracy, or M3C real-time performance.

## VSL-1A conclusion

`0921.mp4` is suitable for an initial **offline** monocular-localization
experiment and for selecting controlled calibration sequences. Its regular
encoded timing, complete decode, broad feature availability, and measurable
motion make it useful evidence. The uneven lower-frame texture and intervals
with low sharpness/high flow mean it should not be treated as a uniformly
qualified sequence; future experiments should retain the interval annotations
and evaluate a controlled, directly recorded sequence as well.

Proceeding to calibration is justified. Proceeding to any SLAM baseline should
remain offline and explicitly encoded-video based until VSL-1B supplies raw
sequence/PTS/receive metadata. VSL-1A is therefore `passed` for its stated
scope, while camera qualification, SLAM qualification, ExternalNav, and flight
qualification remain unproven.
