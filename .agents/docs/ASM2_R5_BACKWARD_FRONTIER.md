Completed 2026-11-13 Asia/Shanghai. **Exactly 20 new native rollouts: 16 qualified, two forbidden-airframe first-manifold failures, and two footprint failures. Zero PPO transitions.** The sampled 3/3 entry extends backward by 20–25 ms in four training sources. The fifth source has a new single nominal success 5 ms earlier, but its 20 ms-earlier triplet fails 0/3.

**Conclusion:** a small earlier autonomous transition support is observed, with complete physical trajectories now available. ## Continuity and available lead time

The tested `dev_near_slow` sequence is **702 pass, 706 fail, 707 archived 3/3 pass**. A single “earlier fails / later succeeds” threshold is therefore inadequate even along this one saved source path. This does not establish that successful regions are disconnected in the full physical state space; alternate paths and intermediate frames remain untested.

For `stationary_0`, nominal frame 327 fails while 330 and archived 331 succeed. These observations bracket a transition over 15 ms of source time, but frames 328–329 were not evaluated here, so a sharp boundary location cannot be claimed. The failed 327 triplet also shows that the chosen slope offsets do not rescue that start.

## Which physical variables distinguish the cases?

Each sample records full target-frame position/velocity, quaternion, body rates, rotor thrust, held targets, finite-mesh separation and resulting contact. The small dataset does not isolate a causal variable or support fitting a reachability classifier.

| Nominal state | Outcome | Slope velocity (m/s) | Alignment error | Body-rate norm (rad/s) | Finite target gap |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| near_slow:702 | Qualified | 0.8153 | 20.967° | 3.2002 | 61.004 mm |
| near_slow:706 | Body present at first touch | 0.7924 | 17.377° | 3.0523 | 51.470 mm |
| stationary_0:327 | Body first manifold | 1.2717 | 32.722° | 2.1344 | 363.356 mm |
| stationary_0:330 | Qualified | 1.2834 | 30.871° | 2.1708 | 341.754 mm |

The near-slow failure starts **better aligned, slower and with lower rate** than the earlier successful state. Simple monotonic alignment, speed or body-rate thresholds therefore do not explain its qualification. Its first signed manifold contains `collision_0_330` and rear-patch contacts; the body point is about 0.454 µm into the native manifold, around target slope/across `[−30.26, +41.80] mm`. This is the unchanged scorer’s simultaneous-contact rejection, not a new continuous-contact-order or loaded-tail conclusion.

The stationary failure starts farther from the target and slightly less aligned; rates, velocity, rotor thrust and motor tracking residual also differ. The nominal trial contacts body collider `collision_0_570` near slope/across `[+50.12, −43.97] mm`. Both perturbations instead first touch front patches outside the registered footprint. Their slope coordinates include +45.46–46.82 mm for minus and +50.12–50.50 mm for plus, beyond the original ±45 mm slope half-width. This is terminal interception sensitivity, despite successful recentering and deceleration.

At near-slow 702, actual rotor thrust is approximately `[0.36073, 0.36047, 0.37119, 0.37026] N`; at 706 it is `[0.35804, 0.35761, 0.37612, 0.37525] N`. The target-minus-actual residual changes as well. These changes co-vary with pose and velocity, so **actuator causality is unresolved**. No actuator-memory experiment was repeated. At each perturbed triplet, initial attitude, rates and all actuator values are identical; only the specified position/velocity pair changes. Four triplets retain qualification, while stationary_0 remains unqualified with a changed rejection type.

**Useful support:** four sampled slope-perturbation triplets and five newly recorded successful autonomous nominal transitions are now available. **Remaining gap:** every selected start already has target-slope velocity directed back toward the target. These records do not establish autonomous outward rotation/reversal, a connected multidimensional entry region, or a directional potential from the original early resets. They should support later dynamics validation, not be converted directly into a similarity reward.

## Exactly one recommended next experiment — not launched

**One ten-rollout cross-track sensitivity test at the five earliest newly successful nominal starts** (725, 816, 702, 330, 346 respectively). Apply the same ±0.5 mm and ±5 mm/s numerical offsets along the *other* target tangent, hold the archived attitude/rates/actuator initial state fixed, and let frozen Fixed A act autonomously. Use these saved nominal outcomes as controls; no nominal rerun or reward fitting is needed.

The current evidence samples source time and the slope direction only, while the terminal failures involve substantial cross-track displacement. This small test would determine whether the new success support tolerates transverse variation or is confined to a narrow path. It reuses the current recorder/scorer with low implementation cost and at most ten additional native continuations, and provides more defensible local support for a future learning method than either extrapolating a region from 3/3 slope tests or training the rejected R4 potential. **Recommendation only; no further experiment has been launched.**
